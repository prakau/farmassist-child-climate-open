# UNICEF Venture Fund application summary

## Challenge and proposed solution

Climate stress and unreliable connectivity can constrain environmental monitoring in school, community nutrition, and vulnerable smallholder gardens. FarmAssist is an early offline-first open-source reference implementation designed to support trained adults with non-personal observations and explainable local checks.

## Technology and current status

The alpha combines a TypeScript PWA queue, FastAPI/SQLite service, JSON Schema, deterministic configurable thresholds, aggregate public indicators, synthetic data, and automated tests. JOITA has an offline-first FarmAssist test build and agronomic workflows; programmer-led development; field-research workflows across three farmer locations covering approximately 25 acres in Haryana; laboratory and field-validation capacity; AgriTrust edge-hardware concepts; industrial-design applications relating to field hardware; and AI, IoT, agronomy, and climate-resilient agriculture experience. These do not constitute validation of this UNICEF-specific module.

## Open source and safeguarding

Software is MIT; documentation/data are CC BY 4.0. No child or personal farmer data are collected. Any pilot requires informed consent, safeguarding and institutional approval, minimization, role-based access, retention, incident procedures, and suitable partners.

## Proposed USD 100,000 use and outputs

Requested funding: **USD 100,000**. Proposed outputs are a validated architecture and governance package, offline application, IoT/sync reference adapters, accessible/localizable dashboard, supervised-pilot toolkit, training materials, evaluation package, independent-review preparation, and a proposed stable release. See [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md).

## Measurable indicators and limitations

Proposed technical and future usability indicators are defined in [monitoring and evaluation](monitoring-evaluation.md). No current values, accuracy, yield, nutrition, health, sensor-performance, revenue, active school pilot, UNICEF endorsement, selection, partnership, or funding are claimed. Thresholds and child-impact hypotheses require independent validation.
